# Optimal market-neutral multivariate pair trading on the cryptocurrency platform

###### Abstract

This research proposes a novel arbitrage approach in multivariate pair trading, termed the Optimal Trading Technique (OTT). We present a method for selectively forming a “bucket” of fiat currencies anchored to cryptocurrency for monitoring and exploiting trading opportunities simultaneously. To address quantitative conflicts from multiple trading signals, a novel bi-objective convex optimization formulation is designed to balance investor preferences between profitability and risk tolerance. This process includes tunable parameters such as volatility penalties and action thresholds. In experiments conducted in the cryptocurrency market from 2020 to 2022, which encompassed a vigorous bull-run followed by a bear-run, the OTT achieved an annualized profit of 15.49%. Further experiments in bull, bear, and full-cycle market conditions demonstrated that OTT consistently achieves stable profits across various market conditions. Additionally, OTT’s arbitrage operation offers a new trading perspective, requiring no external shorting and avoiding intermediate cryptocurrency holdings during the arbitrage period.

## 1 Introduction

The secondary market functions as a modern, efficient form of double auction where participants intending to trade assets submit their acceptable bid and ask prices. The highest bid and lowest ask are matched if the prices meet. We believe in the secondary market’s efficiency (Fama [1970](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib13)), but historical economic bubbles show that investors do not always act rationally. Dale
et al. ([2005](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib8)) illustrated how an order book filled with irrational bids and asks could drive prices in the wrong direction. Identifying inefficiencies in an efficient market helps maintain trading equilibrium. Market directional trading involves astute traders estimating a more accurate price for instruments and placing orders expecting the market to revert to rationality. Traders who bet correctly are rewarded by the market mechanism, contributing to price discovery. Quantitative trading, instead of relying on experienced accountants or financial analysts, utilizes statistics and optimization for better price estimations.

The cryptocurrency market is an emerging secondary market. The recent cryptocurrency market trend benefits from technological advancements and global participation. Its volatility, liquidity, and 24/7 trading make it an ideal platform for algorithmic trading. Users can trade between cryptocurrencies and fiat currencies, combining forex and cryptocurrency volatility. Profits can be generated by exploiting inefficiencies and capturing anomalous movements.

Our research focuses on Pair trading, a widespread strategy based on the Law of One Price (Law of one price (LOP)), suggesting that homogeneous items should have the same price everywhere. However, real-world restrictions like trade frictions and legal regulations often violate this principle (Isard [1977](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib18); Miljkovic [1999](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib23)). Similar assets in a pair should have similar price trends, but their pricing independence can lead to divergence, with convergence often yielding profit. Arbitrage-based trading approaches, grounded in LOP, are perceived as having more stable profitability, favored by investors. Investment banks and hedge funds have used pair trading for decades (Gatev
et al. [2006](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib17); Elliott
et al. [2005](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib10); Burgess [2000](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib3)). While pair trading is common, the extension of pair trading to many pairs (multivariate) trading is not well studied. This paper delves into pair trading and introduces an optimization-based technique for multivariate assets, tested in the cryptocurrency market.

This research has several novel contributions:
① Unlike classical techniques (Perlin [2007](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib26); Galenko
et al. [2012](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib16); Dunis and
Ho [2005](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib9)), our approach uses optimization to arbitrage multiple pairs simultaneously to the individual asset level without grouping assets.
② The bi-objective optimization is adjustable to investor preferences, including tunable parameters like risk tolerance and operating thresholds for maximizing return and minimizing risk.
③ Our arbitrage approach avoids holding volatile intermediate cryptocurrency, maintaining positions in fiat currencies to lower risk.
④ No borrowing is required for shorting, as all trading actions are achievable within the initial principal.
⑤ The proposed technique is robust, market-neutral, and profitable in both bull and bear markets.

## 2 Related Work

### 2.1 Pair Trading

Long-Term Capital Management L.P. (LTCM)was a highly-leveraged hedge fund founded by John Meriwether in 1994 that operated on bond pair trading. It is famous for its billions of dollars in bailout after the 1997 financial crisis and is known as an early real-life example of pair trading (Slivinski [2009](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib29)).

![Refer to caption](extracted/5748609/pairtrading.png)

The operation of pair trading is straightforward. Figure [1](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#S2.F1 "Figure 1 ‣ 2.1 Pair Trading ‣ 2 Related Work ‣ Optimal market-neutral multivariate pair trading on the cryptocurrency platform") illustrates the most cited baseline pair trading framework (Gatev
et al. [2006](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib17)), known as the Distance Method. The common trading terminology includes Long positions as buying assets to sell later at a higher price, and Short as borrowing and selling assets, intending to buy them back at a lower price. We can summarize pair trading in five steps:

Exhaustively search for two assets with the closest historical price movements, y1 and y2. Group y1 and y2 as a Trading Pair.

Calculate the price difference between y1 and y2 as Distance. Set the position opening threshold as two times the historical standard deviation of the Distance (shown as the band area with y1) and the price crossing as the closing threshold.

If y2’s price surpasses the threshold, open a position by shorting y2 and longing y1. Wait until y1 and y2 prices cross, then close the position by reversing the buy and sell actions.

If y2’s price falls below the threshold, do the opposite: long y2 and short y1 until the next crossing point.

Repeat this practice until the end of the trading period.

A critical property of pair trading is achieving Market-Neutrality by betting equivalent amounts on the long and short positions. Since the loss on one position is compensated by the other position, the portfolio value does not move along with the overall market direction. Chen
et al. ([2018](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib6)) formed pairs from asset returns instead of prices, suggesting that a temporarily weaker return implies a stronger rebound, and vice versa. Both Gatev
et al. ([2006](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib17)) and Chen
et al. ([2018](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib6)) based their work on correlation.

Besides correlation, cointegration is a key concept in time-series analysis. Cointegration represents a long-run relationship between two time-series data sets (Engle and
Granger [1991](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib11)). Vidyamurthy ([2004](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib31)) examined assets for cointegration using the Engle-Granger test and combined assets based on correlation and cointegration, providing guidance for cointegration-based pair trading.

Further studies discuss multivariate pair trading, hedging market risk by managing positions and creating artificial pairs from scenarios with more than two assets. Perlin ([2007](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib26)) developed a technique combining assets into an artificial index to form a pair. ### 2.2 Cryptocurrency

The work in Fang et al. ([2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib14)) provides a comprehensive survey of the history and recent research on cryptocurrencies. Cryptocurrency began with the groundbreaking work of Nakamoto ([2008](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib24)), introducing a decentralized monetary system. Decentralization stems from a peer-to-peer ledger recording system. By incentivizing Bitcoin users to record a full transaction ledger on their own computers, the ledgers are distributed across countless individual nodes instead of a centralized server. This property enables frictionless international participation in Bitcoin. Furthermore, global participation provides excellent liquidity for Bitcoin trades. Shortly after, Bitcoin’s success led to the creation of imitators. Ethereum (ETH), the second most famous cryptocurrency, was created by Buterin ([2014](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib4)). Ethereum is considered the next generation of blockchain due to its programmable extensibility.

Popular pair trading techniques from Section [2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#S2.SS1 "2.1 Pair Trading ‣ 2 Related Work ‣ Optimal market-neutral multivariate pair trading on the cryptocurrency platform") have also been introduced in the cryptocurrency market. Fil and
Kristoufek ([2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib15)) applied the distance method based on the work of Gatev
et al. ([2006](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib17)), achieving a 3% monthly profit in the cryptocurrency market. van\_den\_broek\_cointegration-based\_2018 formed pairs based on 60-day trading periods to profit from arbitrage opportunities, demonstrating the inefficiency of the cryptocurrency market.

Similar to gold, ETH has a global market against various fiat currencies. ETH can be traded independently with most major fiat currencies. And the unsynchronization of ETH against different fiat currencies is the inefficiencies in the market that provide trading opportunities. There are some advantages of picking ETH as an anchor over gold because it is completely fungible and has low international transaction frictions. As shown in Figure [2](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#S2.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 2.2 Cryptocurrency ‣ 2 Related Work ‣ Optimal market-neutral multivariate pair trading on the cryptocurrency platform"), the CAD, EUR, GBP, and USD follow a similar trend but do not have 100% correlation. Though the cryptocurrency system operates in a decentralized manner, users can deposit their Bitcoin or Ethereum into a centralized exchange for trading. The exchange functions similarly to stock or forex exchanges, allowing users to freely exchange cryptocurrency for another cryptocurrency or fiat currency, either on spot or margin trading. Due to the decentralized nature of participants, cryptocurrency exchanges operate 24/7 with liquidity coming from global participation. These properties have led to experiments on cryptocurrency exchanges since as early as 2014 (Shah and

## 3 Methodology

There are two stages in our trading technique. The first stage involves screening the assets. We select assets that are correlated and cointegrated with each other to participate in the asset trading bucket. Once the bucket is formed, we monitor the differences between each asset and conduct trades based on opening and closing trading signals.

### 3.1 Screening

![Refer to caption](extracted/5748609/AUDNZDJPY.png)

In the screening stage, we analyze historical data to ensure the assets are suitable for arbitrage. Since movements are impacted by the volatility of both cryptocurrency and fiat currency, severe inflation or deflation in a fiat currency could mislead our trading signals. Therefore, one major criterion is that the volatility of fiat currencies should be small compared to that of cryptocurrency. An example of strong fiat depreciation is illustrated in Figure [3](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#S3.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 3.1 Screening ‣ 3 Methodology ‣ Optimal market-neutral multivariate pair trading on the cryptocurrency platform"): the difference between AUD and NZD is relatively stable compared to the rapid drop of JPY, which would send a short signal based on its value change without mean reversion. To select appropriate fiat currencies for the trading bucket, we reviewed the historical trends of fiat currencies to ensure they have the desired statistical relationships.

The first desired statistical property is correlation. We examine correlation using Pearson’s correlation coefficient (Cohen et al. [2009](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib7)), which ranges between [−1,1]11[-1,1][ - 1 , 1 ]. The higher the absolute value of the coefficient, the stronger the association between the two variables. A coefficient of -1 represents an absolute negative association, 1 represents an absolute positive association, and 0 indicates no association between datasets.

The second desired statistical property is cointegration. We refer to the price difference between two assets as spread. In the long term, a widening spread exposes arbitrageurs to bankruptcy risk. Therefore, it is essential that the spread between any two assets has a constant mean and variance. A time-series dataset with these characteristics is called stationary. Cointegration describes the relationship between two time-series datasets whose difference is stationary. Cointegration is vital in pair trading because forming a pair with cointegration in the cryptocurrency market means that longing one asset and shorting another effectively holds the price difference. If the spread is stationary with a constant mean, convergence should always follow divergence. This property is called mean-reversion. In this research, the Engle-Granger test (Engle and
Granger [1987](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib12)) is used to examine the cointegration relationship.

#### 3.2.2 Buy/Sell Signaling

Arbitrage relies on anomalies. We extract anomalies by comparing the normalized spread with the standard deviation. We use two parameters, Open\_Threshold and Close\_Threshold, to tune the trading signals. For a two-asset trading scenario, the trading process between assets i𝑖iitalic\_i and j𝑗jitalic\_j is as follows:

Calculate the difference of the logarithmized Price to obtain the Spread array.

Normalize the spread array using the Z-Score.

Open a new position if there is no existing position and the Z-score >>> Open\_Threshold ×\times× Standard Deviation.

Close the position if there is an open position and the Z-score <<< Close\_Threshold ×\times× Standard Deviation.

Repeat until the end of the arbitrage period.

There are more benefits to including multivariate assets. If a fiat currency receives a long signal from one pair and an equivalent short signal from another, the actions can cancel out on paper, saving fees. Furthermore, when one fiat currency reveals a strong anomalous movement, all relevant pairs will display signals for the principal flowing in or out of that fiat currency. If there is an entry signal in USD:GBP, all the USD can be used to conduct the operation, ensuring no USD sits idle. Though ideally, this mitigation saves on transaction fees, signals that cancel each other are rare. More often, we have multiple short and long signals happening simultaneously. Additionally, one short signal may come from a position closure and another from a new position opening. Therefore, we need to devise a mechanism to determine how funds should be distributed precisely.

### 3.3 An Optimal Approach to Capital Allocation

Resource distribution addresses how much to invest in each asset whenever a trading opportunity arises. At every trading interval, as long as a trading opportunity is detected, the optimization process is executed to make a quantitative decision on the amount to invest. Optimization is unnecessary if we only trade a two-asset pair since all the capital (or a pre-determined amount) can be allocated for the long and short positions. However, with more than two assets, the multilateral relationships necessitate ensuring that the resources distributed on each signal result in the best outcome. The optimization process aims to maximize return with minimum risk under given constraints. In this research, a bi-objective optimization formulation inspired by the Markowitz portfolio allocation (Markowitz [1952](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib22)) is used to find the optimal investment solution.

### 5.5 Strategy Indicators

To have an overview of the trading strategy, we also look into the trading metrics, not just the profitability and risk. One action of trade is a one-time position opening or closing, which consists of two buy or sell orders. Therefore, the relationship can be presented in an equation:
1 Arbitrage = 2 Trades [position open/close] = 4 Orders [buy/sell].

Table [10](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#S5.T10 "Table 10 ‣ 5.5 Strategy Indicators ‣ 5 Experiment ‣ Optimal market-neutral multivariate pair trading on the cryptocurrency platform") includes some common trading strategy indicators. It consists of three sets of experiments: Full-Cycle, Bull, and Bear markets based on Section [5.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#S5.SS4 "5.4 Profit and Loss Results ‣ 5 Experiment ‣ Optimal market-neutral multivariate pair trading on the cryptocurrency platform").

number of trades: The total number of trades happened during the whole arbitrage period

% winning: The percentage of winning trades among all trades

% losing: The percentage of losing trades among all trades

% long wins: The percentage of winning trades among all long trades

% short wins: The percentage of winning trades among all short trades

Win/Loss ratio: The ratio of winning trades against losing trades

Average loss: The average USD loss among losing trades

Average Win: The average USD win among winning trades

Largest loss: The largest USD loss among losing trades

Largest win: The largest USD win among winning trades

Avg holding hrs: on average how many hours is an arbitrage open

We can clearly see that our technique conducts trades much more frequently than the Distance Method. This is mainly because our spread focuses on the deviation of forex movements anchored to ETH, while the Distance Method concentrates on the distance. Our six experimental setups have a Win/Loss ratio greater than 1 (Table [10](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#S5.T10 "Table 10 ‣ 5.5 Strategy Indicators ‣ 5 Experiment ‣ Optimal market-neutral multivariate pair trading on the cryptocurrency platform")), forming the foundation of pair trading profitability. It is reasonable that the longer the interval, the fewer trades occur, resulting in longer average holding hours. We are also pleased to see that there are no particularly large values in the largest win or largest loss that could impact the overall revenue. This indicates the stability of our arbitrage strategy.

## 6 Conclusion

In this paper, we examined the feasibility and profitability of multivariate pair trading. We used fiat currencies quoted for ETH as the bucket of our pairs. We constructed the spread focusing on anomalies in forex movements due to independent trades against cryptocurrencies. The trading threshold is a tunable parameter based on the standard deviation of spreads. We demonstrated a grid search result of profitability under untuned conditions. In addition to the conventional pair trading strategy, we also introduced an optimization process for resource allocation in multivariate pair trading, ensuring market neutrality and utilizing most of the holding assets without leverage.

The results are encouraging. The profitability of the proposed Optimal Trading Technique (OTT)is on average 2.72 times555The average calculation in the conclusion section is based on 1min, 5min, and 60min sampled datasets for bull, bear, and full-cycle markets under a 0.1% transaction fee more profitable compared to the baseline approach—Distance Method. This implies that trading spread is more effective than distance in the volatile cryptocurrency market. Under the assumption of a 4% risk-free interest rate, the Sharpe Ratio of OTT achieved is a strong 1.17. For all the tested cases, OTT achieved a Win/Loss ratio greater than 1, which drives steady capital growth.

Our bi-criteria optimization objective can be tuned with the risk tolerance parameter λ𝜆\lambdaitalic\_λ, which impacts the optimization process for how much risk a trader tolerates. For the full-cycle market, 5min sampled dataset, the high (λ=2𝜆2\lambda=2italic\_λ = 2), intermediate (λ=1𝜆1\lambda=1italic\_λ = 1), and low (λ=0.5𝜆0.5\lambda=0.5italic\_λ = 0.5) risk aversion parameter values have annualized returns of 7.74%, 15.49%, and 37.74%, respectively. The corresponding Sharpe Ratios of 0.44, 0.68, and 1.11 show a negative correlation with λ𝜆\lambdaitalic\_λ, indicating that strong profitability compensates for the volatility brought by riskier investment choices.

Our Optimal Trading Technique produces positive returns under both bull and bear market trends. In the bull market of 2021, OTT conducted 10,456 trades, while in the bear market of 2022, OTT conducted only 3,306 trades. Clearly, the quantity and quality of arbitrage opportunities are better in a bull market.

Despite the promising results, several limitations should be noted. Cryptocurrency carries significant risks for investors, as highlighted by several sources. For instance, Swaminathan
Anklesaria Aiyar ([2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib30)) likens the cryptocurrency market to the Dutch Tulip Bulb Bubble of the 17th century, where tulip bulb prices soared to extraordinary levels before collapsing dramatically. Additionally, many have documented the risks associated with cryptocurrency investments (Kerr
et al. [2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib20); Angerer et al. [2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib1); Pakhnenko
et al. [2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib25); LEE
et al. [2018](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib21); Jing
Pan [2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.15461v3#bib.bib19)). We use cryptocurrency merely as an intermediate for pair trading fiat currencies due to its liquidity, 24/7 trading time, easy access to multiple fiat currencies in one market, and low barrier for data access. We do not promote investment in cryptocurrencies. Additionally, we selected two years of high-frequency data as our test data source, covering a full-cycle market, including a bull-run and a bear-run. However, this timeframe coincides with the COVID-19 period, during which fiat currencies were also volatile, contributing to spread movement. Profitability might differ in less volatile market conditions. Future work could extend the technique to model the spread from the perspective of the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process to deepen the understanding of the market stochastic process.
